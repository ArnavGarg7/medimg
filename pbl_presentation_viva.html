01 Executive Project Summary & Clinical Problem

In dermatological healthcare, early detection of malignant melanoma is life-critical, with 5-year survival dropping from 99% (localized) to <30% (metastatic). However, access to board-certified dermatologists is bottlenecked by severe clinical shortages, leading to fatal diagnostic delays.

Core Engineering Solution: A triple-headed Software-as-a-Medical-Device (SaMD) clinical workstation prototype built on PyTorch and pure NumPy that provides:
  1. High-Sensitivity Triage Classification: Pretrained ResNet-18 calibrated at a 95% validation-targeted sensitivity threshold.
  2. Lesion Boundary Segmentation: ResNetUNet (ResNet-34 encoder) producing pixel-level contour overlays.
  3. Suspicious Foci Detection: RetinaNet (Focal Loss) extracting localized bounding boxes.
  4. Explainable AI Reporting: Grad-CAM visual heatmaps, zero-hallucination factual clinical summaries, and an ONNX CPU runtime engine.

03 First-Principles NumPy Mathematics (`src/medimg/fundamentals/`)

Unlike projects that only call library wrappers, this project built the core mathematical operations in pure NumPy to prove foundational deep learning competence.

• Analytic Backprop vs PyTorch Autograd

We derived analytic gradients for affine layers, BCE loss, and MSE loss. Central finite-difference numerical verification:

\(\frac{\partial L}{\partial \theta_i} \approx \frac{L(\theta_i + \epsilon) - L(\theta_i - \epsilon)}{2\epsilon}\)

Unit test `test_gradients_vs_torch_autograd` passes with absolute gradient error strictly < 1.0 × 10-9.

• Jacobi SVD & Unsupervised Learning Rules

Implemented cyclical 2-sided Jacobi SVD in pure NumPy without LAPACK. Orthogonal Givens rotations diagonalize symmetric \(A^T A\):

\(\begin{bmatrix} c & s \\ -s & c \end{bmatrix}^T \begin{bmatrix} a_{pp} & a_{pq} \\ a_{pq} & a_{qq} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} c & s \\ -s & c \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} d_1 & 0 \\ 0 & d_2 \end{bmatrix}\)

Verified against `scipy.linalg.svd` to machine precision (error < 10-13). Also implemented Oja's rule: \(\Delta w = \eta y (x - y w)\), proving convergence to the principal eigenvector of the data covariance matrix.

04 Real Clinical Data Curation & Leakage Prevention

The evaluation guidelines prioritize: "Explain dataset origin, size, labels, quality, preprocessing, augmentation, train/validation/test split, and leakage prevention."

• The HAM10000 Data Leakage Trap

HAM10000 contains 10,015 dermatoscopic images, but only 7,470 unique lesions:

  • 1,956 lesions were photographed multiple times across patient visits (up to 6 photos per lesion).
  • A standard random `train_test_split` assigns different photos of the same lesion to both train and test.
  • This causes catastrophic identity memorization, artificially inflating test accuracy while collapsing on unseen clinical patients.
• Engineering Solution: Grouped Stratification

We developed a custom preprocessor (`src/medimg/data_ham10000.py`) that:

  1. Groups by `lesion_id` so that every image of a lesion belongs to exactly one split.
  2. Runs dual perceptual hash deduplication (Hamming distance ≤ 12 + pixel MAD ≤ 0.02) to prune near-identical crops.
  3. Derives detection bounding boxes from ISIC 2018 Task 1 binary masks using connected component analysis (≥ 36 px threshold).

07 Safety Audits, Clinical Robustness & Interpretability
• Grad-CAM Explainability & Failure Audit

We hooked the final residual block (`model.layer4[-1]`) to compute gradient-weighted feature attribution:

\(\alpha_k^c = \frac{1}{Z}\sum_i \sum_j \frac{\partial y^c}{\partial A_{ij}^k}, \quad L_{\text{Grad-CAM}}^c = \text{ReLU}\left(\sum_k \alpha_k^c A^k\right)\)
  • True Positives: Heatmap intensely concentrates on irregular pigment networks and border notches.
  • Disclosed Subgroup Failure: Facial lesions suffered from a drop in specificity to 35.71% because actinic keratosis and solar lentigines mimic melanoma borders under dermoscopy.

• Generative Modeling & Language Safety Audits

1. ConvVAE Minority Augmentation: Trained a convolutional VAE on the minority melanoma class in the train split. Performed an automated nearest-neighbor L2 copy audit, confirming generated synthetic samples were novel interpolations and not direct copies of training data.

2. Autoregressive Transformer Hallucination Audit: We trained an autoregressive Transformer decoder to verbalize clinical reports from model numbers. The Transformer hallucinated—verbalizing an input risk of 92% as "95% melanoma risk" in prose. Because numerical hallucination in SaMD is a fatal safety hazard, we rejected generative language models and mandated a deterministic rule-based verbalizer for the production workstation.

08 4-Minute Technical Viva Defense: Top 10 Anticipated Questions

Click any question below to reveal the ideal conceptual, mathematical, and code-grounded answer to defend during your viva.

Q1: Why did you use transfer learning instead of training your own CNN from scratch? Architecture
"Dermatological images require detecting low-level textural gradients (pigment lines, border asymmetry) up to high-level lesion topology. In our controlled ablation experiments on identical splits, training ResNet-18 from scratch achieved only 0.8363 ± 0.012 AUROC, whereas ImageNet pretraining achieved 0.8984 ± 0.007. Transfer learning acts as an inductive regularizer, preventing overfitting on the minority melanoma class (~11% of the dataset)."
Q2: Explain the exact mechanism by which data leakage was prevented. Data Engineering
"HAM10000 has 10,015 images but only 7,470 unique lesions—meaning nearly 2,000 lesions were photographed multiple times across patient visits. A naive random split places different photos of the exact same lesion in both train and test, yielding artificially inflated metrics due to patient identity memorization. We implemented grouped stratification by `lesion_id` combined with perceptual hash deduplication (Hamming ≤ 12, pixel MAD ≤ 0.02). We verified with unit tests (`tests/test_metrics_data.py`) that the intersection of lesion IDs between train, val, and test is strictly empty."
Q3: Why is accuracy a misleading metric here, and how did you select your decision threshold? Evaluation
"In our dataset, melanoma represents only ~11% of samples. A trivial dummy model that predicts 'benign' for every image would achieve ~89% accuracy while having a 0% sensitivity—killing every melanoma patient. Instead, we used AUROC, sensitivity, specificity, and Dice score. Furthermore, we did not use the arbitrary 0.5 threshold; we selected our operating threshold (\(\tau = 0.518\)) on the validation set to guarantee \(\ge 95\%\) sensitivity, and only then evaluated it once on the test set, achieving 95.24% sensitivity."
Q4: What is the mathematical difference between Momentum and Nesterov Accelerated Gradient? Mathematics
"In our NumPy implementation (`mlp.py`), standard Momentum accumulates velocity \(v_{t+1} = \gamma v_t + \eta \nabla L(\theta_t)\) and updates \(\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - v_{t+1}\). In contrast, Nesterov calculates the gradient not at the current position, but at the 'look-ahead' position: \(\nabla L(\theta_t - \gamma v_t)\). This look-ahead gradient provides an anticipatory damping force that prevents overshooting sharp minima, which we demonstrated in our fundamentals ablation experiments."
Q5: How does Grad-CAM compute activation maps mathematically? Mathematics
"Grad-CAM computes the gradient of the target class score \(y^c\) with respect to feature map activations \(A^k\) of the final conv layer: \(\alpha_k^c = \frac{1}{Z} \sum_i \sum_j \frac{\partial y^c}{\partial A_{ij}^k}\). These weights capture the importance of feature map \(k\). We then take a weighted linear combination and apply ReLU: \(L_{\text{Grad-CAM}}^c = \text{ReLU}\left(\sum_k \alpha_k^c A^k\right)\), isolating features that positively support the melanoma prediction."
Q6: Why did you use Focal Loss in RetinaNet instead of standard Cross-Entropy? Mathematics
"In single-stage object detection, anchor grids generate over 100,000 candidate boxes per image, where 99.9% are easy background negatives. While each negative produces a small loss, their cumulative sum overwhelms the gradients from true lesion targets. Focal Loss introduces a modulating factor \((1 - p_t)^\gamma\) with \(\gamma=2.0\). When a background box is easily classified (\(p_t \approx 1\)), \((1 - p_t)^2 \to 0\), down-weighting easy negatives by up to 100x and focusing training on hard foreground lesion boundaries."
Q7: What is your model's biggest weakness or failure mode? Error Analysis
"Our Grad-CAM subgroup analysis revealed that facial lesions suffer from low specificity (35.71%) because chronic sun exposure induces solar lentigines and actinic keratoses that exhibit pigment network disruption similar to early melanoma. In addition, our robustness testing showed that underexposure below 0.6x normal lighting causes sensitivity to drop from 95.2% to 81.0%. This is why our clinical recommendation is a 'Conditional Go' with strict illumination protocols."
Q8: Why did you reject an autoregressive Transformer for generating clinical reports? NLP & Safety
"In Task C, we trained an autoregressive Transformer decoder to verbalize model numbers into text. During our automated factual consistency audit, the Transformer suffered from numeric hallucination—for example, converting an input risk of 92% into '95% melanoma risk' in the text. In Software-as-a-Medical-Device, numerical discrepancies in triage reports can lead to improper clinical pathways. We therefore mandated a deterministic, rule-based clinical verbalizer for production."
